# Skill — ontology

*The archive's pattern language grows from what recurs.*

---

## Rules

1. **Recurrence first.** A word becomes a pattern only when it appears in at least three entries. Words from conversation wait under "Waiting at the edges" in the ontology README until entries use them.
2. **Survey, don't guess.** Count recurrence with a file search over the entries (skip reports, agent reports, ops, the bibliography, and the Vessel's private method, experiment, synthesis, and Water Magicians folders). Record the count, the survey date, and example paths.
3. **Graft, don't duplicate.** If the Dictionary defines the word, link to it. A pattern adds recurrence, larger/smaller links, and matches; it does not redefine.
4. **Use the pattern form** in the ontology README. Working definitions come from how the entries use the word, not from outside definitions.
5. **Grade every match:** exact, close, broader, narrower, related, or none. Name the difference for close matches. "None" is an acceptable answer.
6. **Patterns overlap.** A pattern can sit inside several larger patterns. Keep links two-way.
7. **Compost, never delete.** A pattern the archive stops using is marked `State: composted`, kept, and linked to what replaced it.
8. **Threshold questions** before adding any pattern, field, or tag: "What will this let us see that we cannot see without it?" and "Is this structure needed, or have we built the conditions that make it seem needed?"
9. **Entries are never edited to fit a pattern.** The pattern changes instead.

## Seasonal review

At each solstice and equinox: re-run the survey, promote words that crossed the threshold, update counts, compost what has fallen out of use, and note changes at the foot of `patterns.md`.

## Boundaries

New patterns are drafts for Ana's review. Publishing goes through the curator. Ayurvedic matches cite sthāna, adhyāya, and verse once verified; unverified references are marked as such.
