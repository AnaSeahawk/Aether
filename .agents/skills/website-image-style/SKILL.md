# Website image style

Start with the reader's moment. On a card they are choosing where to go. At the page title they are finding their bearings. Beside a paragraph they may be able to see in one glance what would otherwise take several sentences. Open the current page, follow the link if there is one, and look at the art in place before proposing a change.

## Choose what carries the meaning

- **A thumbnail and its destination.** Keep the name legible as page text. Reuse the approved thumbnail wherever an illustrated link leads to that destination. The same mark can appear at the title. When the full emblem invites a closer look, draw a simpler thumbnail with the same silhouette, color relationships and principal subjects in the same positions.
- **An illustration that carries words.** Name the thought someone should understand at a glance. Cover the caption briefly and describe what you see. A few words can finish the thought. In Place Readings, the order from basket to board to pot to bowl to rack lets the eye follow a meal. In A Website of One’s Own, two women with cups of tea convey the ease of a first conversation. Each style fits its subject.
- **Art specific to a piece.** The individual illustrations in essays, including the longer Sovereign Biophysics pieces, belong to those works. Keep a navigation review focused on the links and entry points Ana identifies.

## Work from an approved reference

Ask what an existing image already does well, or recover Ana's stated reason from the brief. Borrow the relevant quality. The Living Year gave Urbaculture a seasonal color wheel and circular form. Those choices express a relationship between the two spaces. Other subjects can have their own palette and silhouette.

The approved Urbaculture pair is in `Components/website/assets/urbaculture/`. Open `urbaculture-wren.webp` and `urbaculture-roundel.webp` together. The roof draining into the barrel beside the greens shows how the city garden works. The flower and visiting wren show who else can live there. Enlarging the wren against dark green made that idea legible on a phone. When simplifying, keep a detail that carries part of the message. Give it space or contrast and remove competing detail first.

## Review one reader journey

1. Identify the link, its destination, the current assets, and the idea or shape the reader should recognize on arrival. Reuse a working mark when it already does this.
2. Preview a proposed change on the actual card and at the destination heading. Use the dimensions of that placement. The Writing card currently uses 80 px on mobile and 120 px on desktop. Inspect both at normal viewing size and in light and dark themes.
3. Look away and glance back. What reads first? Which details merge? Follow the link. Is it immediately clear where you landed? Keep an accessible link name and describe meaningful destination artwork in its context.
4. Show Ana the pair for a decision. Record the selected assets and the reason worth carrying forward. Apply approval to that pair and its agreed placements. After release, follow the live link on a phone-sized screen.
