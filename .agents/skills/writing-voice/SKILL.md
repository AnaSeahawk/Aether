# Skill — writing-voice

*Distil the essence, then write it well: mysterious, romantic and clear.*

Load this together with `prose`. `prose` holds the craft rules. This skill holds
the voice those rules serve.

---

## Edit, don't transcribe

Ana speaks her thoughts by dictation, following what she feels. Her words are
raw material, not finished text. Her work goes through an editor, the same as
any writer's.

- Find the essence and write it in this voice. Don't keep her phrasing word for
  word.
- The facts are hers, and so is the choice of what stays private. A detail she
  asks to leave out stays out.
- The sentences are the editor's craft. Never offer to "put your words in
  exactly".
- When Ana says that a passage is finished in her own words, it is finished.

This is different from contributor observations in the Water of Life, which
keep the contributor's own first-person voice (see `water-of-life`).

---

## The form: the living inquiry essay

Philosophical creative nonfiction and literary nature writing, with room for
symbolism, history, observation through the body, and the occasional poetic
passage. It is never academic, conventional memoir, or self-help.

- Begin with something that really happened.
- Stay close to what was seen, felt and done.
- Bring in history or other views only where they shed light.
- Let a question stay open. Not every piece needs to resolve.
- Invite the reader into the discovery. Don't tell them what to think.

Keep it personal without confessing, philosophical without drifting into
abstraction, and poetic without losing accuracy.

---

## Spare, like Hemingway

Ana likes Hemingway. Keep sentences short and plain, and keep the pace moving.

- Cut anything long-winded. A life story is a handful of scenes, not a
  chronicle. Choose the scenes that carry feeling and let the rest go.
- Turn toward meaning rarely: one line now and then, earned by the scene
  before it.
- Weave credentials in as facts within the story, never as a list. Example:
  "certified as a diamond grader by the Gemological Institute of America", set
  inside the goldsmith passage. Her formal training in looking closely belongs
  in the work.
- On the About page and in other writing about her, use the third person. In
  essays, keep "I" and "you" sparse.

an author. It is part of the process through which experience takes form.

## Six disciplines to borrow

Borrow each writer's discipline, not their sound:

| Author | Book to start with | Discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Nan Shepherd | *The Living Mountain* | Attention through the body to a landscape known from within |
| Annie Dillard | *Pilgrim at Tinker Creek* | Following an observation into the unknown |
| Kathleen Jamie | *Findings* | Restraint and precision; not everything needs explaining |
| Ursula K. Le Guin | *The Wave in the Mind* | Independent thinking, with warmth and discipline |
| Rebecca Solnit | *A Field Guide to Getting Lost* | The associative essay; at home in the unknown |
| Clarice Lispector | *Água Viva* | The language of inner experience |

Shepherd and Dillard form the central pair: quiet embodied attention against a
searching, expansive imagination.

## Touchstone

This passage from the About page set the direction on 21 November
2026:

> In Yellowknife the basement became a goldsmith's workshop. Her father had
> gone back to school for the craft, and she learned it at his bench. Every
> piece began in wax. The wax forms were joined into a tree, sealed in plaster
> and fired until the wax ran out, leaving only its shape behind. Gold from the
> crucible was spun into that hollow. Something had to vanish before anything
> could be made.

It works through short sentences, real materials, the process followed step by
step, and a single line that opens into meaning.
