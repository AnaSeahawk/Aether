# Interview Guide — The Water of Life Archive

For in-person or remote conversations that will be recorded, transcribed, and
formatted into archive entries.

---

## Before the interview

1. **Explain the archive.** The contributor should understand what this is:
   an open observational archive, not a medical study, book, or advocacy
   project. Their words will be publicly accessible.

2. **Identity choice.** Ask how they want to be identified: real name,
   pseudonym, or anonymous. This decision is permanent for their entry.

3. **Consent.** Confirm they understand the entry will be public and that
   the archive makes no medical claims.

4. **Recording.** Confirm they consent to being recorded. Explain that the
   audio is retained privately, only the transcript is published.

---

## The conversation

These are not rigid questions. They are doors. Let the person talk. Follow
what matters to them. The template sections will be filled from what they
share naturally.

### Opening

*"Tell me about your experience. Start wherever feels right to you."*

If they need more direction:

*"What was going on in your life when you first came to this practice?"*

### What they were noticing

*"Before you started, what were you paying attention to in your body or
your life? What was drawing your attention?"*

Avoid: "What was your condition?" / "What were you diagnosed with?"

If they volunteer a diagnosis, let them — it is their word, not yours. Do
not repeat it back as a category.

### Discovery

*"How did you first hear about this? What made you actually try it?"*

### Their practice

*"Walk me through what you actually do. Whatever you are comfortable
sharing."*

Follow-ups if needed:
- *"How often?"*
- *"Has it changed over time?"*
- *"Is there a particular routine or timing?"*

### The full picture

This is the most important section to cover thoroughly.

*"During the time you have been doing this — what else has been going on?
Other things you have been doing for your health, changes in your diet,
your work, your stress levels, anything."*

If they say "nothing else" — gently probe:
*"Same diet? Same exercise? Same sleep? Same medications or supplements?
Nothing changed at work or at home?"*

The point is not to undermine their experience. It is to give it context.

### What they observed

*"What have you noticed? What changed, what did not change, what
surprised you?"*

Let them describe in their own words. Do not lead toward positive or
negative outcomes.

If they make a causal claim ("it cured my..."), do not correct them in
the moment. Let them speak. The transcript preserves their words. The
structured entry will reframe into observational language.

### Timeline

*"When did you start? How long has it been? Were there phases —
a beginning that felt different from now?"*

### Open space

*"Is there anything I have not asked that you want to say? Anything
about this experience that does not fit into what we have talked about?"*

---

## After the interview

1. Retain the audio recording privately.
2. Transcribe using AI transcription tools.
3. Format into an archive entry following the entry format in the skill file.
4. If the contributor is named or using a pseudonym, share the draft with
   them for review before publishing.
5. Anonymous entries are reviewed by the archive steward only.

---

## Notes for the interviewer

- You are not a therapist, doctor, or authority. You are a listener.
- Do not validate or invalidate what they share. Hold it.
- If they cry, let them. If they laugh, laugh with them.
- People have often never told anyone about this practice. Give them room.
- Your job is to help them say what is true for them, not to shape their
  story into something the archive wants to hear.
