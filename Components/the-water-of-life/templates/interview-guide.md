# Conversation Guide — The Water of Life Archive

For recorded conversations that will be transcribed and formatted into
archive observations.

---

## Before the conversation

1. **Explain the archive.** A public observational archive, not a
   medical study or advocacy project. Their words will be publicly
   accessible.

2. **Display name.** Ask how they want to appear: a name, a nickname,
   or anonymous (the default).

3. **Consent.** Confirm they understand the observation will be public
   and that the archive makes no medical claims.

4. **Recording.** Confirm they consent to being recorded. The audio is
   stored privately until the transcript is reviewed and approved, then
   deleted. Only the transcript enters the archive.

---

## The conversation

This is not a questionnaire. It is a conversation. Let the person talk.
Follow what matters to them.

### Opening

*"Tell me about your experience. Start wherever feels right to you."*

### The three things you need to understand

Somewhere naturally in the conversation, make sure you understand:

1. **What were you doing?** — which pathways, how long, how often
2. **What did you notice?** — changes, non-changes, surprises
3. **What else was happening?** — concurrent factors, other treatments,
   diet, stress, medications, environment

If these come up naturally, don't interrupt to ask again. If they
haven't come up by the end, ask.

### If they make causal claims

Let them speak. The transcript preserves their words. The formatted
observation will use observational language — "noticed," "observed,"
"during this period." Share the draft with them before publishing so
they can see how their words were reframed and approve it.

### Closing

*"Is there anything I haven't asked that you want to say?"*

---

## After the conversation

1. Store the recording privately.
2. Transcribe.
3. Format into an observation following the archive's observational
   language.
4. Share the draft with the contributor for review before publishing.
5. Once approved, delete the audio recording.

---

## Notes

- You are not a therapist, doctor, or authority. You are a listener.
- Do not validate or invalidate what they share. Hold it.
- If they cry, let them. If they laugh, laugh with them.
- People have often never told anyone about this practice. Give them room.
- Your job is to help them say what is true for them, not to shape their
  story into something the archive wants to hear.
