# Web design: less reading, more seeing

26 November 2026 · anaseahawk.com

## The idea

The site carries long, rich writing. The new pages (A Website of One's Own, Conversations & Circles) show the other half: a picture says what a paragraph would, and a small movement draws the eye back. The aim across the whole site is two things:

1. **Fewer words where a picture can carry the meaning.** Offer pages, Circle pages and the Living Year should be readable at a glance.
2. **Rests inside the long reads.** Where the writing stays long (the essays, Sovereign Biophysics), a picture every few screens gives the eye a place to rest.

One rule makes it work: **the same mark always means the same thing.** The sprout is spring wherever it appears. The little Sun on its circle is Keeping the Year. The ring of dots is Gather, the speech bubbles are Talk and the open book is Read. Once a visitor learns a mark, it guides them without words.

## How heavy is too heavy

| What | Cost to the visitor | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Drawn marks and their movement (SVG and CSS) | almost nothing, a few KB | Use freely; this is the house style. |
| Still pictures (WebP) | 20–80 KB each at the sizes used | Fine; load below-the-fold pictures only as the reader reaches them. |
| A short video loop | 1–3 MB each | Sparingly: one per page at most, e.g. the knocking-door clip. |
| Voice recordings | nothing until pressed; about 0.5 MB per minute | Safe; the file loads only when someone presses play. |
| Big animation libraries | 100 KB and up | Not needed; avoid. |

Every movement stops for visitors who have turned on reduced motion on their device. Movement belongs at the edges of reading (headings, cards, the space between sections), never inside a paragraph being read.

## Voice on the page

Yes, this is possible and light. A small "Listen" button at the top of a page plays Ana speaking about what the visitor is about to meet: one or two minutes, unscripted, in her own voice. Nothing loads until it is pressed. Recordings can be made on a phone; they would be trimmed, levelled and compressed here. Good first places:

- **About**: the life, in her voice.
- **Shivambu room**: a welcome into the subject.
- **Each Circle page**: what happens in the Circle.
- **The Living Year**: how to inhabit time.

Read-aloud versions of the long essays could follow later.

## What keeps people coming back

This is from general knowledge of the web, not measured data:

- **A voice and a face.** Sites that are clearly one person's (personal newsletters, illustrated essay sites, "digital gardens") keep readers because they come for the person. The faces on the Women's Circle card work the same way.
- **A rhythm.** The new-moon letter already gives one. A small "this lunation" note on the home page would show the site is alive.
- **Something to return to.** The sky line at the top changes every day. The Living Year's calendar and the season marks change through the year.
- **Pictures that explain** (illustrated explainers) are shared far more than text alone.

Few, if any, sites combine a living sky-calendar, hand-made drawings and a single practitioner's voice. That difference is the strength.

**Home.** Use the season marks and door marks so the three doors read at a glance. A "this lunation" strip (the current Moon and one line) would show the site is alive.

**The Living Year** (about 1,100 words). Replace the two Circles paragraphs with the speaking and listening pictures plus one line each, as on Conversations & Circles. Shorten each season card to the name and a few words, as on Conversations & Circles, and let each Circle's own page carry the detail. Add a small moving Moon beside "On the calendar".

**Keeping the Year** (about 1,200 words). Already rich in photos. Draw "What the Circle holds" as four small pictures instead of a list.

**The Living Waters, First Waters, Edges and Play** (the Circle pages). Each gets a hero picture and its season mark. First Waters and Edges and Play have no pictures yet: two ChatGPT illustrations in the house style would carry them.

**Shivambu room** (no pictures). A welcome picture, and later a short voice welcome.

**Ways of Living, Urbaculture, Place Readings, A Room for Living.** Place Readings has no pictures: a drawn before-and-after of a room (plants, light, air) would explain it at a glance. Urbaculture's plates are wonderful and could be animated gently: the soil cycle as a moving loop of bokashi, fly and humanure.

**Sovereign Biophysics.** Keep the long reads, but add a picture every few screens as a rest. The Tortoise in the Bowl already does this well (15 pictures); it is the model.

**About** (about 1,100 words, 7 pictures). Already visual; a voice recording would make it the warmest page on the site.

**Foundations: Freedom Is Love** (about 1,500 words, no pictures). One or two archive photographs of Helen Humphreys Seargeant's papers, if any can be shared.

**The Phoenix** (about 2,900 words, drawn figures). The figures carry it; a short summary at the top would help newcomers.
