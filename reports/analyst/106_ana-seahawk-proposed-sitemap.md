## Editorial basis

The tree maps proposed reading paths and distinct spaces. It is not a mirror
of Git. Repository visibility does not determine website inclusion: public
repository material may remain outside the website, and private material may
become a website piece after selection, preparation, and release approval.

Each branch needs a clear purpose, a relationship to its parent, and actual
material to support it. Unfinished, abandoned, or unplaced material remains in
the working inventory. Even within a named collection, inclusion is selective.

The site shares Ana's knowledge and point of view without deciding why visitors
have arrived or what they should seek.

## Homepage and connections

The homepage holds Ana's introduction and presents these destinations.
Contact, occasional updates, and the sitemap provide practical navigation.
The three independent sites receive equal visual presence. The Women's Circle
is planned as its own site; its domain and internal page structure remain open.

Connections can cross collections without duplicating them. A plant essay can
link into Sovereign Biophysics, a personal journal entry can link to The Living
Year, and the Vivarium can appear in the backstory with a link to its ecological
writings.

Ways of Living > Urbiculture > Relationship with Plants is an agreed direction.
The guiding sentence's placement beneath Ways of Living remains a proposal.
This tree describes destinations, not a requirement to put every branch in the
top navigation.

## Material without an assigned website destination

Foundations, developing manuscripts, historical programmes, business
possibilities, and miscellaneous creative work remain in the working inventory
for individual editorial decisions. Their omission from this tree is not a
deletion or a judgment that they can never belong. No category is created merely
to accommodate a file.
