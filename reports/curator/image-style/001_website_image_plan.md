# 001. A plan for website thumbnails and logos

**Date:** 2026-11-27  
**Agent:** Codex  
**Session topic:** Building recognition across website links and entry pages

A reader who chooses a picture on Writing should recognize the place they enter. Urbaculture now gives us a working example. The small wren, growing greens and roofline on the card recur in the detailed garden emblem at the title. A visitor can recognize the garden before taking time to notice the compost, bee and water cycle.

## Where to go next

1. **Walk the other Writing links once.** Start with a phone-sized view. For each illustrated link, note the destination, what catches the eye on the card, and what the reader recognizes on arrival. Mark a pair as working when the connection is already clear. A name and a well-placed existing illustration may do the whole job.
2. **Choose one gap with Ana.** Bring the card and its destination into view together. Prefer a place where a familiar image disappears on arrival or where the thumbnail becomes hard to read. First try reusing the existing mark or simplifying its small version. Let the destination supply the colors and subjects.
3. **Compare the pair at reading size.** Show the phone card, desktop card and page heading. Ask what the image says before explaining the drawing. Use Ana’s response to decide which detail earns its space. After approval and release, verify the click and record the two asset paths and the reason for the choice.
4. **Extend an established mark when the same destination comes up again.** Keep the same cue across illustrated links. Revisit this plan as examples accumulate. The review can end whenever the reader’s path already feels clear.

Place Readings and Conversations are references for a different job. Their pictures can replace an explanation or convey the character of an encounter. Keep those successful examples available when designing an explanatory image. The artwork inside individual essays stays with those pieces.
