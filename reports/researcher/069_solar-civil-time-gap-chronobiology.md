## What was found

The Living Year already held the distinction between civil and solar time as
an orientation. Chronobiology holds the same distinction empirically and names
both the mechanism and the strain.

- The body's ~24-hour cycle, entrained primarily by light, is the **circadian
  rhythm**.
- The discrepancy between social and biological time is **social jetlag**,
  coined by Till Roenneberg and colleagues, 2006.
- The field frames it as **three clocks** — social (local time), sun (light and
  dark), biological (physiology). This is the archive's own distinction,
  reached independently and from the other direction.
- Spain moved GMT → CET on 16 March 1940 to align with Berlin and never
  reversed it, though most of the country lies west of Greenwich.

Solar noon on the Andalusian coast (36.72ºN 4.42ºW) falls near 13:17 midwinter
and 14:19 high summer — a gap of 1h17m to 2h19m from clock noon. Computed, not
cited; recompute against an ephemeris before public use.

## Editorial limit

The literature associates sustained social jetlag with metabolic,
cardiovascular and depressive outcomes. None of that list is reproduced in the
journal or the module. The journal names the gap, gives the reader the means to
measure their own, and stops. Keep that limit if this material is extended.
