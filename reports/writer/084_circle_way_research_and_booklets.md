## The Circle Way — research summary

The Circle Way is a group-conversation form developed by Christina Baldwin
and Ann Linnea (PeerSpirit Inc., from 1992), documented in *The Circle Way:
A Leader in Every Chair* (2010) and *Calling the Circle* (Baldwin). The
canonical free reference is the two-page "Basic Guidelines for Calling a
Circle" at thecircleway.net.

Components, per the official guidelines:

- **Intention** — shapes who comes, how long, and what outcomes to expect.
  The caller articulates intention and invitation before anything else.
- **Start-point / welcome** — a gesture that shifts attention from social
  space to council space (silence, a poem, a song).
- **Setting the center** — the "hub of the wheel"; objects that represent
  the intention (candle, flowers, a bowl).
- **Check-in / greeting** — a brief round, usually volunteer-first, that
  makes everyone present; passing is allowed and the turn returns later.
- **Agreements** — commonly: confidentiality; listening with compassion and
  curiosity; ask for what we need, offer what we can; a guardian may pause
  the circle.
- **Three principles** — leadership rotates; responsibility for quality is
  shared; reliance is on wholeness rather than any personal agenda.
- **Three practices** — speak with intention; listen with attention; tend
  the well-being of the circle.
- **Forms of council** — talking-piece (slow, collect all voices),
  conversation (interaction), reflection/silence.
- **Guardian** — one member watches group energy and may ring a pause; any
  member may request one.
- **Check-out / farewell** — each person names what they learned or what
  stays with them; a formal release from council space.

**Roles.** The Circle Way names host (calls the circle, sets intention,
leads as a peer), guardian (watches energy, rings the pause), and scribe
(keeps the record). This matches the "leader in every chair" principle:
roles rotate rather than belonging to individuals.

**Role-specific materials.** The Circle Way publishes free booklets at
different depths rather than one document: the two-page Basic Guidelines
(participant level), The Circle Way Pocket Guide (structure, agreements,
troubleshooting; Baldwin & Linnea), a Hosting Guide for Virtual Circles
(host/guardian tips, sample online session flows), and deeper booklets on
shadow/projection and energetics in groups. See
https://www.thecircleway.net/booklets. This supports a future separate
**hosts' companion booklet** for when the roles open beyond the founding
three — the July 24 facilitator flow is its natural spine.

## Adaptation choices (for review)

- **Attribution** kept in both booklets ("adapted with gratitude from The
  Circle Way… thecircleway.net"). The guidelines document is offered
  freely, but the books are copyrighted; the booklets paraphrase rather
  than quote, except the three practices/principles wording, which is
  short and attributed.
- **Talking piece** is impractical on a video call; the shivambu booklet
  uses structured rounds plus the raise-hand queue, and the Mother Spirit
  booklet names the three council forms (round, open conversation,
  silence) in plain language.
- **Camera-on minimum** from the held circle is preserved in the shivambu
  booklet only; the Mother Spirit call has no such rule on record, so its
  booklet asks only for a name and one word.
- **Scribe confidentiality**: the booklet states the scribe's record "stays
  within the circle's keeping," consistent with the private archive lane
  and the media-consent log.
- Medical-disclaimer and sovereignty language carried into both, per
  `skills/sensitive-content.md` and Megan's safety section.
